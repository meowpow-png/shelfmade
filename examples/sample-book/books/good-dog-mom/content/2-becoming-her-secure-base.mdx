---
title: "Becoming Her Secure Base"
order: 2
---

Developmental psychologists have a name for what every good dog mom already does instinctively: she becomes a secure base. A secure base is not a cage, a leash, or a rule. It is a person reliable enough that the dog can wander toward the world and know, without checking twice, that home hasn't moved.

Consistency builds that reliability faster than any single training session. The same calm tone after a mistake. The same patient hand through a thunderstorm. The same unhurried walk out the front door, every single day, whether or not anyone is in the mood for it. Dogs do not need their person to be perfect. They need their person to be predictable.

<Insight>
  Anxiety in dogs, as in children, rarely comes from too many rules. It comes from too much
  uncertainty about who is in charge of keeping things safe. Calm leadership is not about control —
  it's about removing that question entirely.
</Insight>

When she looks back at you mid-walk, at the edge of a crowded park, that glance is the whole relationship in miniature: a quick check that the base is still there. Meet it, and she'll keep exploring.
